How much does a logo cost in 2026? Real prices and how not to waste your money

The question every business owner searches before starting. Here are the real ranges, what makes the price go up, and the traps people fall into when they try to save the wrong way.

If you're opening a business — a restaurant, a store, a medical practice, a workshop — at some point you've asked: how much does a logo really cost? You'll find answers from $5 to $50,000, all sounding plausible. Here's what the market actually looks like, no smoke.

Three levels exist in 2026. First, the template logo ($5 to $30): someone picks a Canva template or an icon bank and swaps in your name. Fast and cheap, but one important thing: that same template is being used by another business somewhere else, and cheap icon banks don't always come with clear commercial licensing. If you grow, that logo can become a legal headache.

Second, custom design ($50 to $300 in Latin America): a real designer studies your business, your competition and your audience, and creates something that exists only for you. This is where 90% of serious businesses live. Price goes up with what's included: social media versions, color palette, licensed fonts, editable files.

Third, the full identity ($300 to $1,500+): not just the logo — the whole system, how your brand looks on a menu, a billboard, a uniform, on Instagram. For businesses with several service lines or franchise plans, this is what keeps the business from looking put together from leftovers.

Why not just ask your cousin with free Photoshop? Because design isn't the file, it's the process: knowing the audience, testing contrast on a printed menu at 8pm under tube light, checking the logo still reads from 50 meters away at the billboard, delivering the right files for each use. 'Looks nice' is free; what you pay for is that it works.

Signs you're being charged fairly: they ask questions about your business before drawing; they deliver transparent PNG plus vertical and horizontal versions; they ask where you'll use it (print, social, billboard) because that changes how files are prepared.

Signs you're paying for smoke: they promise 20 options in 24 hours; the 'concept' is copied from Pinterest; you can't talk to the person designing. Extreme speed almost always means a template with another name.

Our advice if you're starting out: don't spend money you don't have on a $1,500 identity, but don't burn yourself with a $5 logo you'll have to redo when you grow. Start with well-thought custom design and build from there. Rebranding later costs more than getting it right the first time.
